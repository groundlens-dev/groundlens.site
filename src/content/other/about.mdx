**Groundlens** is a research project by [Javier Marin](https://www.linkedin.com/in/javiermarin/) focused on triage methods for LLM outputs in production — *pay immediate attention to particular priorities*, deterministically.

The core question: can you rank LLM responses by grounding signal without asking another LLM to judge them?

The answer is yes, within a boundary we can now draw exactly.

Embedding geometry separates a response that disengaged from its source from one that did the work. It does not separate a confabulation that stays *in register* from a correct answer: as the wrong answer adopts the right topic, the right terminology and the right structure, every embedding-similarity method declines toward chance. Controlling for who wrote the text, the ceiling of the whole class is in the high 0.6s AUROC. That is not a weak estimator. It is the ceiling.

Entailment models do not decline there. They are the best thing available in-register. So Groundlens is a first stage, and the boundary is written into the product rather than around it.

## Research

Three papers form the foundation:

1. **Semantic Grounding Index** (arXiv:2512.13771) — ratio-based grounding verification for RAG systems.

2. **A Geometric Taxonomy of Hallucinations** (arXiv:2602.13224v3) — three-type hallucination classification. The confabulation benchmark.

3. **Rotational Dynamics of Factual Constraint Processing** (arXiv:2603.13259) — transformers reject wrong answers via rotation, not rescaling. 

## Philosophy

groundlens is verification triage, not truth detection. It tells you which responses earned the right to be trusted and which need human review.

The methods are deterministic. The same input produces the same score every time. Auditable by design.
